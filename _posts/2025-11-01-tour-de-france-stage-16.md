*How to beat a GOAT of the sport*
The Tour De France 2023 was one of the most exciting races in recent years. Coming into Stage 16, the top two leaders (Tadej Pogacar and Jonas Vingegaard) were separated by only 10 seconds, one of the closest margins in Tour history.
First, a little bit of background. The Tour De France is the hardest sporting event in any discipline. It’s 3 weeks of 100 miles per day, with mountains that can go up to 10k feet of elevation gain… and then you do it again the next day. Also it’s 90 degrees Farenheit with 80% humidity. It’s tough. People compare racing a Grand Tour to like, dying. It’s a rough race, and it’s impressive that anyone can do that.
The top two people in the race are Tadej Pogacar and Jonas Vingegaard. Tadej Pogacar… is the next GOAT of cycling. This guy, at 23, has won 2 Tour De France and a number of 1-day stage races. He was dominating pro cycling at 21! Over the past 2 years, he’s mopped the field in the Tour De France. In 2020, he beat the reigning favorite, Primoz Roglic, on the final Time Trial to win the TdF.
![Tadej Pogacar in 2021.](/images/essays/notion-2.png)
In this race, he’s been second though, to Jonas Vingegaard. Jonas isn’t a chump himself. He had won last year’s Tour De France, with the help of his team, Jumbo-Visma. They had gone on an all out attack on Col du Granon, with multiple teammates helping him, to drop Tadej and gain 2 minutes on him. And that’s all he could get between the two. Famously, he worked in a fish factory while he was in the junior leagues. But, he has an absurd VO(2) max (97!) and a tolerance for pain that very few can match.
![Tadej and Jonas in 2022](/images/essays/notion-3.png)
In the 2023 Tour De France, after Stage 15, Jonas leads Tadej by 10 seconds. And everyone expects Tadej to win the Time Trial. Jonas, while a good cyclist, isn’t the best time trialer, and he can’t put in as much absolute power as Pogacar can. People expect it to be close, but a victory for Tadej in the end.
The course itself was one that favored climbers. It’s a hilly course, with a 9% average grade 3k climb near the end. One tactic used here is to swap the TT bike for a road bike, which theoretically should get you to climb faster, since heavier bike == slower to pedal up a steep hill.
![](/images/essays/notion-4.png)
Race day comes. Tadej is chilling, doing backflips into the team pool. Some people are out on the course, doing some practice laps. Jonas is observed checking every corner, doing a ton of practice on the course, trying to take lines as optimally as possible.
Race day comes. Remy Cavagna takes an early lead, with Wout Van Aert taking the hot seat about 15 seconds ahead of him. No one really comes close to either minus a few of the mid-top-10 riders, with Pedro Bilbao being the best of the bunch.
Tadej Pogacar comes down, and he’s flying, beating everyone to the first and second checkpoints. He’s dialed in. Nothing can stop him. He changes his TT bike for a road bike for the climb, a 9% brutal but thankfully short climb. He’s so fast, he even catches his 2 minute man Carlos Rodriguez to smoke Wout Van Aert by nearly 1:15 minutes.
![](/images/essays/notion-5.png)
Finally, down comes Jonas Vingegaard. He has 10 seconds on the GOAT of the sport, and he knows he needs to ride this perfectly in order to stand any chance at beating him. He comes down the ramp and you can tell, from the first corner, he’s putting in work.
![](/images/essays/notion-6.png)
First checkpoint arrives, and he’s 30 seconds ahead. Ahead of who? Wout? Tadej? It turns out, he was 30 seconds ahead of Tadej, who was already stupid ahead of everyone else. The announcers are in disbelief. “They’ll be some nervous, nervous people — look at that! I’m not even going to finish the phrase. 30 seconds ahead of Pogacar now — if they’re not panicking in the team car maybe they should start to!”
He maintains his 30s lead up to the start of the climb, where the overhead camera expects him to change bikes, but he keeps going. He hits the climb on a TT bike, and it’s miserable. But *he keeps gaining time* *on Pogacar.* At the third checkpoint at the top of the climb, he’s a *minute* ahead of Pogacar. And he keeps gaining time.
He hits the finish line, and he is dead. Look at this guy and tell me that’s not someone who gave it everything.
![](/images/essays/notion-7.png)
The final verdict? 1:38 ahead of Pogacar. Absolute domination. He was further ahead to Pogacar than Pogacar was to third place. Wout van Aert, his teammates, tips his hat to him. And Jonas isn’t a good TT rider. He’s a climber at his core, so this performance came out of nowhere.
That TT performance won him the Tour de France. The next day, Pogacar cracked on Col de la Loze and the Tour was over. But I think this was probably the best stage of Vingegaard’s career. Dominating everyone with absolute willpower on a course that was super hard and beating the GOAT while he was at it? Amazing.
