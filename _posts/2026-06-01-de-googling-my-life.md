In 2004 GMail launched. 1GB of email storage space. Free. Web hosted. At the time, the coolest thing I had ever seen. Invite-only because doing webmail in 2004 required racks and racks of physical servers. Changed the internet forever.
In 2026, things are a little different. Google is a monopoly player, dominant in search. Gmail is everywhere and is the second most used email client. Google has Android, Photos, Calendar, and a whole bunch of services that make it insanely useful to any consumer, almost a requirement.
It also makes it a single point of failure. Access to your email means that you have access to everything — credit reports, banks, logins, health data, photos, docs, anything. It’s kind of worrying. Google support has been notoriously finicky with free accounts, and as a result I’ve become more and more worried over time about something happening to my account.
So how do we migrate off of Gmail? I’m going to list out all of these, and have blog posts detailing how I did each one.
1. Gmail
I’m going to try Proton mail and calendar for a few weeks. I think it’s the best privacy connection, but it is not easy to set up and apparently there’s headaches on the privacy. They also have docs + calendar.
1. Docs
Protonmail has docs, but I’m thinking of a local-first approach to storing my documents. I bought a Beelink ME Pro to store my photos and documents, and use that as my local storage. NextCloud is a FOSS solution to hosting,
1. Photos
Same as documents, use Immich + local hosting to store all my photos with family user accounts.
1. Calendar + Contacts
Not sure on this part yet. Protonmail has calendar. It feels very integrated with email so it might have to be a feature I pay for.
1. Browser
I’ve already mostly switched to Brave browser (which is built on top of Chromium, so I guess it’s not a full de-google-ification) but it’s a pretty nice alternative.
The big things are going to be the local server — that’s gonna take some time to set up and get everything running. I’ll have some tutorials on getting things set up.
